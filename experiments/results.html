The two models. Original: trained on all of Sydney in 5 rounds (5-fold spatial cross-validation), so each area is predicted by a model that never saw it. Good-areas: trained only on the 20 areas that already have the most toilets for how busy they are, then used to predict everywhere else. The original learns "what Sydney normally has"; good-areas learns "what well-served places have".

Reading it: both rank new suburbs well (10× better than random) but weaker than established Sydney (0.31 vs 0.52). 2 · Are its new toilet locations valid places?

In progress. An automated check is classifying every candidate (original 120, good-areas 314) using OpenStreetMap and NSW zoning: is there already a toilet the national map misses, is it a shopping centre, a park / station / beach / library, or just houses or industry? It is calibrated against hexes that really have toilets. This section will be filled in when it finishes. It's a proxy; the human review in candidate_review.csv is still the real test.

What we know already:

  • Good-areas keeps 119 of the original's 120 candidates and adds ~195 new ones. Its candidates are stable: 293–298 of 314 return with a different random seed.
  • The original's top candidates include large shopping centres (e.g. Liverpool, Bondi Junction), which likely have customer toilets the public map doesn't list.
  • In Bradfield (below), both models' top pick is exactly where the master plan places public amenities.

  • The airport terminal scores low (0.57): the model has never seen an airport.

Does the plan-based method work? Oran Park check

We rebuilt Oran Park (already built, 5 real toilets) from zoning only, as if it were unbuilt, and compared with its real features.

InputsPredicted totalRank of the real-toilet hexes

The plan-based method finds the real toilets about as well as real data does, so the Bradfield ranking is credible. Hex-by-hex agreement is only moderate (0.50), and zoning misses small parks.

Leppington Town Centre (4 real toilets) was also run: top pick in both models is the mixed-use hex 126 m from Leppington station.

Recommendation for the demo: use the original for "where toilets usually go" and good-areas as the "raise the standard" view; show the growth-suburb test as evidence it works on new suburbs, and Bradfield as the future-suburb story with its assumptions labelled.

Caveats

  • The models learn existing placement, not need. Good-areas is a higher bar, still learned from today's toilets.
  • Growth suburbs use 2021 population and only the 19 basic inputs.
  • Bradfield shops, cafés and bus stops are assumptions with low/mid/high scenarios; there is no jobs input, and Bradfield is a jobs centre.
  • No public dataset of planned toilets exists; plans were checked by reading the master plan PDFs.
